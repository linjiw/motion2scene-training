## 1. Bottom line

**Tracking.** On tracking completion (repaired 89/20 corpus, seeds 91231 and 91260), release SONIC is the best checkpoint: 83–86/89 train and 17–18/20 dev (inspected) [M, M-doc]. No fine-tune beats it on dev. Whether it can stop at a goal is untested:
- On completed clips, its median end-root XY error is 0.50 m on train and 0.54 m on dev [C, seed 91231].
- Its strict proxy is 12/89 train and 2/20 dev [C, seed 91231].
- It has no root-XY termination.
- It has never been run on the stopping panel.

Phase 0.2 and Phase 1.3 settle this.

**Navigation is the bottleneck.** Its headline number means less than the first roadmap said:
- The approach-weighted DAgger result of 7/19 is in-sample. It was scored on the physics seed (91260) that produced its training labels, and rollouts are bit-deterministic.
- The tasks are timed reproductions of training clips, and no wall forces a route change [M].
- The zero-training stack on disk has never been measured: goal → scripted routing → SONIC's kinematic planner → frozen release SONIC. TANGO 2609.09158 uses nearly this chain (A* → velocity commands → SONIC planner → obstacle-aware editing) to generate training data, but reports no such baseline.

**Novelty has moved.** PASSAGE 2609.18732, MTC 2609.21107, ViBe 2609.09918 and TANGO now cover much of the original plan. Acting in SONIC's token space is not a contribution by itself:
- The public SONIC release documents token-space downstream policies: latent-residual modes and Protocol v4 token streaming.
- Task Tokens 2503.22886 already ran PPO in a frozen BFM's token space.

**Proposed contributions [P].**
- **C1.** A matched comparison of where goal and scene information should enter one frozen SONIC. The four candidates are typed planner commands, a reference chunk passed through the frozen encoder, the 64-D FSQ token, and a decoder LoRA. The benchmark forces route and posture decisions.
- **C2.** A falsifiable test of the imitation gap. Prediction: students distilled from reference-tracking teachers fail in proportion to the route and timing ambiguity that goal and map cannot reveal. Goal-conditioned teachers should not show this, and neither should visibility-masked teachers under perception.
- A token-space goal policy is the default instantiation, not the claim.
- The scripted planner baseline (B3) and the benchmark become a baseline section and an appendix. On their own they are workshop or tech-report material.

**Next two weeks:**
- pre-register the held-out-seed confirmation (Stage 1) and launch it while the GPU is idle (1.9 GB used, 0% utilization at the time of writing);
- read the planner kinematically and posture by posture;
- build the packet for running release on the stopping panel;
- start task schema v2.

The hardware answer (§12 Q1) blocks Phase 3. A weekly arXiv watch with pivots decided in advance runs from day 1 (§8).

## 3. What changed from the first version

1. **"The motor foundation is narrow" was wrong.**
   - The 35/100 and 8/20 figures were on unrepaired references. On repaired data, release SONIC completes 17–18/20 dev, the best of any checkpoint. The gain came from repairing the references, not from fine-tuning.
   - What holds: fine-tuning on 89 clips on a plane never improved dev. Those runs also narrowed the release recipe: plane instead of trimesh terrain, one encoder instead of three, no adaptive sampling.
   - What is open: release's root drift and strict quality are poor (§2). For stop-at-goal tasks these may matter more than completion.
2. **"The student is capped by the teacher" was wrong.** Dev completion is 10.3 for the student, 15.7 for the teacher and 17 for release. The gap is on the student side, and its cause is untested.
3. **Evaluation cost was misstated in both directions.**
   - "About 1 h per panel" was the contended case, and "25 min per task" was a timeout default. The best case is about 11 min per 19-task panel.
   - However, the median measured cost is ≈290 ms per control step, about 6× the best case, so typical panels take 40–60 min.
   - Multi-seed confirmation needs no new harness. The Phase 2 sweep does (§8 1.4).
4. **The 7/19 is in-sample.** The collection seed equals the eval seed (91260), and rollouts are bit-deterministic (maximum prefix difference 0.0 m). Even in-sample, the arm difference has p≈0.125. All seven wins are on tasks of 1.63 m or less.
5. **The tasks are timed reference reproductions, not navigation.** The deadline and the 3-D goal come from the reference, and the walls never force a decision. All 12 motions are train clips from one ancestry group. The 00399-corridor failure is a construction bug, not a policy failure.
6. **"Short-cycle DAgger moved navigation" was only partly true.** Only the approach-weighted arm improved (4→4→7); the uniform arm declined (4→3→2). Both arms share their cycle-1 recovery data.
7. **Residual PPO never ran.** It is also wired to the legacy navigator, not to the 114-D motor.
8. **"Commands barely change actions" is stale.** It was true only for the early MLP. Later models respond to commands offline; closed-loop controllability is still untested.
9. **The SONIC-planner baseline was missing.** The planner ships with the deploy stack, is on this host, and accepts typed commands: mode, speed, movement and facing direction, height, and waypoints. In the deploy gamepad code, height is clamped and applied only in the static modes 4–6.
10. **Teacher action space.** The first version proposed a residual on the 114-D motor command. The 64-D FSQ token, in bounded code space (§7), is now the default instantiation and one of the four interfaces compared in C1.
11. **"Build the vectorized harness first" is replaced.** Sequential panels are enough for Phase 0. Phase 1 builds a multi-instance per-scene evaluation harness before the benchmark lock, and every arm is evaluated in it. The vectorized env is used only for training and dev gates.
12. **Literature has moved.** PASSAGE, MTC, ViBe and TANGO (all September 2026) overlap the original plan. A new flow-matching planner is no longer a contribution by itself. Neither is acting in SONIC's token space, which the SONIC release documents.
13. **Kimodo is blocked on this host.** The code and venv are here but the weights are not. The archived Kimodo crouch variants also qualified poorly (duck 1/24, arm-tuck 0/24).
14. **A plain `RayCasterCfg` height map cannot hit the current obstacles,** which are Cube/Sphere prims. Use `MultiMeshRayCaster` or baked trimesh. A plain 2.5D map also loses the free space under overhangs.
15. **The MID-360 mount is ambiguous.**
    - `vendor/sonic/gear_sonic/data/assets/robot_description/urdf/g1/main.urdf:612` is the URDF spawned in Isaac. It mounts the sensor inverted at torso +0.416 m, with rpy (0, 3.101, 3.1415).
    - `vendor/sonic/decoupled_wbc/control/robot_model/model_data/g1/g1_29dof.urdf:625` mounts it upright at +0.406 m, with pitch 0.04.
16. **Smaller fixes.**
    - "Scripted rays 36/61" was a superseded snapshot; the final figure is 46/72.
    - Only V2 and V3 are evaluation locks, with physics seeds 93101–93102 and 94301–94302. The V3–V6 amendments were proposed but never adopted.
    - Athena-WBC has been withdrawn (§4).

## 5. Diagnosis: the real bottlenecks, ranked

1. **The benchmark cannot measure navigation.**
   - What: the deadline and the 3-D goal come from the reference, no task forces a decision, the motions are in-sample, and the collection seed equals the eval seed.
   - Consequence: no navigation number so far can be read as navigation.
   - Evidence: `scripts/navigation_distill/build_tasks.py`; `direct_context.py:32-60`; `scripts/navigation_distill/dagger_cycle.sh:6,18-19`; the failure breakdown in §2.
2. **The obvious baseline has never been measured.**
   - Consequence: we do not know whether known-map traversal needs learning at all, or where.
   - The planner's low postures:
     - mode 4 (`IDEL_SQUAT`) is a static squat with a height input and cannot move under an overhang;
     - the moving low modes are crawling (8), elbow crawling (14) and the stealth walks (18, 22).
   - The local adapter whitelists only modes {0, 1, 2, 4} and forces `has_specific_target=0`.
   - Crawling puts hands, knees or elbows on the floor. The current scorer terminates on any non-foot floor contact above 1 N and on pelvis z below 0.25 m, so every crawl would score as a failure (`direct_scene_runtime.py:175-186`).
   - Evidence: `/home/robotixx/GR00T-WholeBodyControl/gear_sonic_deploy/src/g1/g1_deploy_onnx_ref/include/localmotion_kplanner.hpp:78-115`; `input_interface/gamepad.hpp:349-383`; `vendor/sonic/gear_sonic/dataset_generation/hallucination/motion2scene_planner_adapter.py:21-40`.
3. **There is no goal-conditioned expert, and the student's target is ill-posed.**
   - The adapter regresses 113 command dims (29 joint positions, 29 joint velocities, 42 keypoints) from goal + map.
   - Its only expert tracks a reference on a nominal clock. The reference's future and timing are not functions of goal, map and history, so DAgger labels average over them. This is the imitation gap analysed by A2D.
   - Consistent with this, but not proven: label yield collapses as the takeover point moves later (18/19 at 15% → 3/19 at 75%), and 0/6 long tasks are solved.
   - Evidence: `navigation_motor.py:51-67`; `workspace/nav-8192/collect/recovery-plan-*.txt`.
4. **The statistics are too weak to rank arms.**
   - There is one eval seed and one training run per arm, and both arms use fit seed 91482.
   - A gate of "mean ≥5/19 over 3 seeds" passes a policy whose true rate is 4/19 about 20% of the time [S; model in §10].
   - A pooled McNemar test over task×seed pairs ignores clustering by task.
   - Evidence: `workspace/nav-8192/dagger/*/c2-fit-config.json`.
5. **The low-clearance motion vocabulary is unproven on the frozen tracker.**
   - The Kimodo crouch edits qualified poorly, and Kimodo weights are absent here.
   - The planner's crawl and stealth modes have never been tracked physically. Their transit speed and stand-to-crawl transition time are unknown.
   - Evidence: `vendor/motion2scene/experiments/registrations/E1_CROUCH_LADDER_Q3_RESULT.md`.
6. **Perception has not started, and the stock sensor geometry is unresolved.**
   - If the inverted mount is real, the body-frame elevation field of view is about −54°…+5° [C].
   - Assume the sensor sits about 1.2 m above the floor while standing [A]. Then an underside 0.1–0.2 m above the sensor leaves the view about 1.1–2.3 m before the robot reaches it [C]. A registered memory would then be mandatory.
   - Phase 0.9 computes this per posture.
7. **Provenance is scattered.**
   - At least 12 docs claim to be the current entry point.
   - The cycle-2 evidence lives only in the gitignored `workspace/`, and the release numbers at seed 91260 are doc-only.
   - Local `main` is 14 commits ahead of `origin` after this revision.

**Research question [P].** On one bit-frozen, off-the-shelf SONIC, where should goal and 3-D scene information enter for whole-body indoor traversal? The candidates are typed commands to SONIC's planner, a kinematic reference chunk through the frozen encoder, the 64-D FSQ token, or a decoder LoRA. A second question is why students distilled from reference-tracking teachers fail. Both are measured on a benchmark that forces route and posture decisions under held-out layouts and physics instances.

**Primary claims [P], conditional on the §8 gates.**
- **C1: interface comparison.**
  - Four learned arms, one per interface (§7), share teacher data, reward and budget.
  - Each arm uses K≥3 training seeds, and K=5 for the top two. All arms run in one evaluation harness.
  - The scripted B3 is the reference point.
  - The benchmark includes a pre-registered expressivity band that lies beyond the planner's vocabulary.
  - Every outcome is reportable: a winner, a tie, or an interface that cannot express a band.
- **C2: imitation-gap mechanism.**
  - Setting 1: F1 mirrored bypass pairs, with controlled route and timing entropy in the references (§8 3d).
  - Setting 2, under perception: full-geometry vs visibility-masked teachers (§8 4.2).
  - The predictions are pre-registered, and a failed prediction is reported as a negative result.
- **Instantiation.**
  - The default method is a token-space goal policy trained by asymmetric PPO with a privileged critic.
  - It is distilled into a MID-360 student by DAgger, then by PPO with a decaying DAgger term.
  - The tracker stays byte-identical and is deployable through the C++ runtime's external `token_state` (Protocol v4).

**Secondary results [P].**
- **S1.** B3 on the decision-forcing benchmark, with each failure attributed to planning or tracking. This becomes a baseline section and a benchmark appendix, not a standalone paper.
- **S2.** For a head-mounted MID-360, a visibility-masked teacher distils better than a full-geometry teacher; this is one of the C2 predictions. A registered memory on its own is standard practice, not a claim.

**Not claimed:**
- the first voxel or goal-conditioned whole-body traversal (MTC, Gallant);
- the first frozen SONIC with perception (ViBe);
- flow planning with a LiDAR multi-layer map (PASSAGE);
- the first policy acting in SONIC's token space (SONIC release, Task Tokens);
- gains from DAgger → PPO (GuideWalk, Perceptive Humanoid Parkour).

**Deviations from the vision, for paper 1 [P].**
- **Masked multi-mode interface: deferred.**
  - The command is the goal only, with validity bits.
  - Measured failures justify the deferral: the 79-D line reached 5–6/89 (14/89 once) with 0/20 dev, and the 114-D line 21/89 with 0/20.
  - What would change on a retry: per-mode task-reward PPO through the frozen decoder instead of supervised reconstruction.
- **Generative planning: reduced to a baseline and data source.**
  - It shrinks to SONIC's shipped planner.
  - The reference-chunk arm tests the chunk principle.
  - A token-space flow student is optional within C2.
- **Real-G1 transfer: still after the method.** The hardware answer gates Phase 3, and a B3 hardware smoke test is scheduled early if a G1 exists (§8 Phase 5).

- **Default tracker.**
  - Release SONIC (`workspace/models/sonic_release.pt`), kept bit-frozen.
  - The 8192×500 checkpoint stays as a comparison arm (B3-8192).
  - The motor student and the navigation adapter are sha-bound to 8192×500 (`anticipatory_motor.py:24-32`). They remain historical baselines on that stack; rebuilding them on release is not budgeted.
- **Token path.**
  - Set `use_student_direct_latent=true` (`manager_env_wrapper.py:104-127, 587-662`). Otherwise the wrapper falls back to `residual` mode (`:719`) and silently adds the policy output to reference tokens.
  - The `mixed` mode (`:774-859`) and the trainer's `meta_action_dim` are the intended DAgger host. They exist but are untested here.
  - `sonic_release/exported/` is empty, so an export step is a prerequisite.
  - Every smoke test asserts that tokens lie on the FSQ lattice and that the actor matches the release actor.
- **Action parametrization.**
  - The installed FSQ computes `tanh(z+shift)·15.5 − offset`, then rounds (`vector_quantize_pytorch/finite_scalar_quantization.py:147-155`).
  - One bin is therefore ≈0.065 in z near zero and much wider near saturation. A fixed Gaussian std in z explores the 64 dimensions very unevenly.
  - Policies therefore act in bounded code space (post-tanh, pre-round, linear bins). Add a wrapper flag that skips the bound and only rounds, or apply atanh first.
  - Noise, std and the §8 0.8 gate are all stated in lattice units.
- **I2.** Reuses AnticipatoryMotor's packing: residuals over current-frame repetition for 9 future frames (576-D), packed to 640-D and fed to the frozen release G1 encoder.
- **I1.** The planner (batch 1, 774 MB, ≈28–33 ms per call) cannot label 2048 envs online.
  - First try editing the ONNX graph to accept a batch dimension (0.5 ED).
  - Otherwise, train a command→token prior offline from CPU kinematic planner rollouts across modes, speeds and directions, encoded with the release G1 encoder (3–5 ED).
  - I1 enters C1 only if the prior passes a fidelity gate (§8 3c).
- **I4.** Follows ViBe's style.
  - The goal enters through a goal-only token stream: the prior applied to B1 commands, or a goal-only I3 policy if the prior fails.
  - The scene enters only through the decoder LoRA.
- **Outputs.** The action is the primary output, with auxiliary heads for clearance, SDF at keypoints and contact risk. No scene reconstruction.
- **Evaluation.** Every arm runs in the Phase 1.4 multi-instance harness, through a callback that decodes policy outputs via the ATM `direct_latent` path.

### Phase 3 — Known-map C1 and C2 (weeks 8–15)

**3a [P] Sampling guidance over the planner** (only for branch (ii)).

- `scripts/research/export_release_atm.py`, with a CPU parity test against the full release actor (<1e-5).
- Goal command, observation, reward and termination terms. Every termination currently in `mdp/terminations.py:59-261` is reference-relative.
- `goal_tiles` terrain through the `SubTerrainBaseCfg.function` hook. The floor and a merged obstacle mesh are two static prims, so contact filters stay uniform across envs. Per-env primitive tensors and an analytic SDF go with it.

- Memory calibration at 1024/2048/4096 envs, with peak ≤13 GB. The 8192-env PPO peaked at 16.9 GiB on a plane, which is over budget.

**3c [P] Known-map policies (C1).**
- **Merged step.** The known-map I3 policy is both the known-map result and the teacher for Phase 4. This merges the old 3b and 4.1: a separate known-map student would have the same inputs, so distilling into it would do nothing.
- **Actor observations:**
  - 930-D proprio history and the last action;
  - body-frame goal with validity bits;
  - local geometry within 4 m (≤8 primitives + SDF at 14 keypoints);
  - base velocity, pelvis height and foot contacts.
- **Critic observations:** everything the actor sees, plus DR parameters, pushes, the full tile and time.
- Neither actor nor critic sees a reference future, clip phase or clip-derived deadline.
- **Rewards:** progress, hold, braking, contact, fall, action rate, cycle residual (I3), plus the existing smoothness terms.
- **Initialization:**
  - BC on B3 data in each arm's own action space, with a decaying BC anchor.
  - 50 critic-only warm-up iterations.
  - Policy std of about 1 lattice bin (I3).
  - Hindsight-goal BC from Kimodo clips is not used for initialization. A clip's path and timing are not a function of its endpoint, so it would reintroduce the old adapter's ill-posed target.
- **Arms.** All share data, reward and budget, with K=3 training seeds each:
  - **I1**, only if the batched prior passes its fidelity gate: release driven by the prior completes ≥90% of held-out planner command sequences on flat ground;
  - **I2**;
  - **I3**;
  - **I4**, with an 89/20 tracking re-run to check that tracking is preserved;
  - **I3 from scratch**, with ≥150M transitions or a pre-registered plateau rule. This tests the Depth Dodge report.
- **Throughput.** Measured: 8.3k transitions/s at 8192 envs on a plane, and 16.4M DAgger transitions in 2,302 s at 2048 envs. With obstacles and a ≤13 GB cap, assume 5–7k transitions/s [A], so 100M transitions ≈ 4–6 GPU-h.

**3d [P] Mechanism test (C2)**, on F1 mirrored bypass pairs, plus F3 where feasible.
- Generate B3 references offline on CPU at three entropy levels: deterministic; left/right 50/50 on mirrored pairs; randomized speed and mode-switch timing.
- The reference-tracking teacher is frozen release SONIC tracking these references in the vectorized env. The references are precomputed, so no online planner is needed.
- The student uses the same network and token output as I3 and is DAgger'd from this teacher at each entropy level (K=3). This is the MTC-style arm: only the teacher type differs.
- Compare it with the same student distilled from the I3 goal policy.
- **Pre-registered prediction:** the reference-distilled student's collision rate rises with entropy, because it averages left and right into the obstacle. The goal-teacher student stays flat.
- Optional: a token-space flow student on the highest-entropy data, as the multimodal fix.
- **DAgger note.** The uniform arm's decline (4→3→2) is hypothesized to come from reference-tracker labels at late or off-reference states (yield 3/19 at 75% takeover). A teacher whose labels are consistent with the student's current state should not decline. Every DAgger run adds a per-cycle non-regression check on held-out tiles.

**Gates:**
- **MVP (I3):** all of the following:
  - ≥60% success on held-out tiles and ≥50% on route-switch tiles;
  - falls ≤5%;
  - shuffling the goal drops success by ≥40 points;
  - a second training seed reaches ≥50%.

## 10. Evaluation protocol

**Units.**
- An episode is one (training run k, layout/task t, physics instance s).
- Instances are shared across arms (same seed, same robot draw), so comparisons are paired.
- All arms, scripted and learned, are evaluated in the Phase 1.4 multi-instance harness. The vectorized training env is used only for dev-tile gates and is linked to the harness by an equivalence probe.
- Method claims need K≥3 training seeds, and K=5 for the headline arms.
- Use S≥3 instances per layout on dev. Confirmation panels are sized by simulating the registered test (placeholder S=8).

**Splits.**
- Layout generator seeds and physics seeds are disjoint across train, dev, confirmation and test.
- Collection seeds come only from the 70000–79999 block.
- The burned-seed list is generated from `workspace/**/command.json`, the config seeds and the lock JSONs, never by hand.
- The test split and the V3 panel are sealed and opened once per finalist arm.
- The family list for any "where B3 fails" claim is recorded in the registry before the test split opens, and every family is reported.
- The 20 dev motions are labelled "dev (inspected)", never "held-out" or "test".

**Metrics.**
- **Primary: contact-free success, per family.** The robot must:
  - reach XY ≤0.25 m and hold for 50 ticks at ≤0.10 m/s;
  - make no obstacle contact >1 N at 200 Hz;
  - make no non-foot floor contact >1 N, except for the bodies whitelisted for that family's posture;
  - not fall (pelvis z <0.25 m for standing families; a torso orientation and height rule for crawl families).
- **Secondary:** success with contacts allowed, time to goal, SPL (geodesic), minimum body clearance, which body made contact, root drift, joint-jerk p95, foot slip.
- Legacy numbers keep a column labelled "legacy (3-D goal, reference deadline)".

**Statistics.**
- Wilson 95% intervals per arm.
- Two-arm binary comparisons use an exact CMH test stratified by task, or a task-cluster bootstrap. Pooled McNemar is secondary only.
- Crossed bootstrap over training runs × layouts, with instances nested.
- Holm correction over the pre-registered family of tests.
- IQM for continuous metrics, computed on subsets where both arms succeed.
- **Simulated power [S].**
  - Model: logistic, with task effects σ 1.0–2.0, arm×task interaction σ 0.5, and instance effects σ 0.5–2.0 shared across arms. The 6 long tasks are fixed at failure.
  - Test: pooled two-sided exact McNemar, 500–1000 repetitions. Scripts will be committed under `experiments/power/`.
  - The results assume true rates of 7/19 vs 4/19. 7/19 is in-sample and probably optimistic.
  - At S=8, power is 0.83–0.99 at α=0.05 and 0.77–0.95 at α=0.025.
  - 5/19 vs 3/19 needs S≈10 for power 0.80, or S≈15 for 0.96.
  - The registered gate has not been simulated yet. It is one-sided, task-stratified CMH, Holm-corrected over two comparators, and requires a mean ≥5/19. Phase 0.1 simulates it before registration, including lower true rates (3–7/19).

**Hygiene.**
- Every result row carries a protocol stamp: seed set, split, input version, harness commit, DR profile, observation-corruption flag, checkpoint sha.
- Gates are pre-registered before running.
- Infrastructure failures (startup OOM, KILL timeout) are rerun at most twice and logged. Policy failures are never rerun.
- Nominal and DR regimes are reported separately.
- Tracking completion is always reported next to root drift and the strict-quality proxy.
