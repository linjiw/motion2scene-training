# Seven-schedule development experiment

The completed 35-branch development acquisition demonstrates different physically
usable responses to overhead constraints. It also establishes a strong simple
baseline: either complete prior-derivative schedule passes all five development
contexts. This acquisition does not establish a passage advantage from learned
selection or a curriculum comparison.

## Executable interface and measured teacher panel

The common frozen tracker consumes a verified bank of four equal-duration
references through seven complete schedules. Neutral has no adaptation; short
and sustained authored crouches can enter at 0.30 or 1.40 seconds; the authored
prior derivative can enter at 0.30 or 1.00 seconds. Short and prior schedules
return at 5.30 seconds, sustained schedules at 5.10 seconds. Each episode allows
at most one adaptation. Repeated decisions while neutral do not imply repeated
adaptations or unrestricted switching.

The neutral source was generated by the installed prior. Short and sustained
references are authored `local_crouch` variants of that neutral source. The prior
alternative is an explicitly authored projection and splice of a separately
generated neural motion; it is not the unmodified generated sample. Promotion
required complete physical entry, maintenance, recovery, and contact evidence in
the verified registry. Equal duration or common seed alone was not qualification.

All 35 forced schedules used physics seed 8731 and the same five explicit
development definitions. The bank loads 299 frames at 50 Hz. Each bounded
invocation records 298 physical control rows and 1,192 physics steps, ending before
reference resampling. The complete panel contains 10,430 aligned sensor/state
packets and 41,720 physical steps. Independent audit retained 35 known outcomes,
including nine failures; there are no unknown branch outcomes in this panel.

The following numbers are measured passage times in seconds for successful full
episodes. `Fail` denotes a known physical task failure, not absent measurement.
The empty scene and obstacle scenes have different declared finish planes, so
absolute times across columns are not one common travel-time task.

The long beam distinguishes short from sustained adaptation. The early beam
distinguishes the authored prior derivative from all short/sustained schedules.
The two-beam course is an integration check that every beam is represented and
measured: neutral passes it too. Its result does not demonstrate that multiple
adaptations are necessary or have been learned. Different legal entry times may
precede identical reference states; their existence alone does not establish a
different physical adaptation onset.

Each teacher decision uses the actual neutral preaction history at ticks 15, 50,
and 70. A candidate continuation is admitted only when its physical state,
actions, reference tokens, and causal sensor history exactly match that prefix.
WAIT is assigned the best measured future complete schedule available from the
same recorded history. The panel has 15 available neutral decision packets and
15 complete legal action tables. Fourteen are consequential for fitting; the
early beam's final phase has only failing available continuations and contributes
no invented successful target.

The student inputs are the same 114 named sensor/state features for every method:
100 floor/ceiling-history and robot-state features, seven active-schedule entries,
and seven legal-action entries. The 65 ideal PhysX rays accumulate two seconds of
history with observed/unknown masks and zero added delay. Ideal query normals are
not measured noisy camera normals. Privileged geometry and physical outcomes
remain supervision and audit data, never policy inputs.

Passage requires the bounded episode's complete physical, command, and contact
checks. All robot bodies have explicit 200 Hz counterpart measurements for the
floor, walls, robot links, and every declared beam. Internal self-contact is
reported separately under the registered criterion; undesired environmental
contact fails the physical task. The two-beam course retains both beam columns,
ordering, and the complete course finish requirement. An unreached second beam
cannot count as course passage.

## Frozen policies for actual development validation

The common ridge penalty was selected through a registered five-layout
development cross-validation using the fixed candidates
`[1e-6, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10]`. The selected penalty is 10. The subsequent frozen
114D phase-conditioned value model was fitted to the complete panel's 14
consequential decisions. Fitting these targets alone does not establish actual
learned traversal performance; the completed executions are reported below.

The 72 registered sensor-script settings were ranked on the same five contexts
using exact pre-entry matching and each first committed branch's actual outcome.
All seven constant complete schedules were ranked on that panel too. The selected
script and constant both have 5/5 successful recorded-branch proxies. Their
respective mean relative-time regrets are 0.00086207 and 0.00145985, arising from
two 0.02-second time differences. The complete ranking and interpretation are in
[the baseline selection note](SEVEN_OPTION_BASELINE_SELECTION_V1.md).

## Completed physical comparison

All 20 assigned invocations completed with 23,840 measured physics steps and
5,960 aligned sensor packets. Each has complete measurements; 17 passed and the
three walking failures remain in the denominator. There are no unknown outcomes.
The independent audit recomputed every stored result with the **original archived
source snapshot**, and reproduced every actual neutral decision from its frozen
model or script inputs. It does not silently substitute a later scorer revision.

| Actual policy, seed 8732 | Empty | Short beam | Long beam | Early beam | Two-beam course | Passage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Learned, five-context fit | 5.38 | 3.60 | 4.18 | 2.72 | 4.12 | 5/5 |
| Selected five-context script | 5.40 | 3.60 | 4.18 | 2.72 | 4.12 | 5/5 |
| Constant prior, entry 0.30 s | 5.38 | 3.60 | 4.18 | 2.72 | 4.12 | 5/5 |
| Always walk | 5.40 | Fail | Fail | Fail | 4.16 | 2/5 |

Values are measured successful passage times in seconds. The learned policy and
constant have identical passage and time results on this panel. The selected
script is 0.02 seconds slower only in the empty scene. This experiment therefore
does **not** show a learned passage or efficiency advantage over the strong
constant baseline. The walking failures have maximum measured undesired beam
forces of 2,969.61 N, 2,241.01 N, and 1,909.36 N for the short, long, and early
beams respectively. None of these three failures involves a recorded fall or
reference reset.

The learned policy chooses the early prior schedule on the short and long beams,
and waits to the late prior schedule on the empty, early-beam, and two-beam
contexts. The script keeps walking in the empty scene and waits to the late prior
schedule on each obstacle context. The constant enters the early prior schedule
in every context. All adaptations complete the declared mandatory return, without
refusal or state-mutating transition. Both beam force streams and complete course
scoring are retained; the course remains passable by walking and does not require
multiple adaptations.

The measured attempt wall time totals 1,771.37 seconds, including simulator
startup and execution; it excludes queue/resource waiting. The 20 invocations
have distinct capture paths, but deterministic dynamics yield six distinct
trajectory content hashes. They are one development seed, not independent
statistical replicates. Earlier teacher times at seed 8731 must not be substituted
for these actual executions.
