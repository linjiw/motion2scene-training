Abstract
Generated whole-body motions paired with scene geometry can supply references for humanoid control, but
successful reference execution does not ensure that an autonomous policy can select the same behavior from a goal
and map. We study this interface using a navigation adapter that predicts a 114-dimensional current command for
a frozen recovered SONIC-based motor specialist. The implemented system preserves the forecaster, reference
encoder, finite scalar quantization, decoder, and normalization, while separating public observations from privileged
command targets. We evaluate structured command supervision, same-state motor-action supervision, and an
optional causal localization-velocity input on eight in-sample stopping and corridor tasks from four clips in one
ancestry group. On the same 3,900 nominal teacher states, motor-action mean squared error decreases from 0.004055
to 0.000296–0.000299, a 13.6–13.7-fold reduction. Nevertheless, the two new adapters complete zero and one
of eight tasks; the selected successful task fails at both additional evaluation seeds. State-preserving takeovers
distinguish timely recovery, stabilization that occurs only after a deadline, and persistent goal drift. These results
show that the measured offline fitting gains do not establish reliable navigation and motivate, without validating,
navigation-induced continuation-qualified supervision. We formulate that extension and a matched-budget study of
generated-pair utility. Reliable held-out navigation, context-dependent route or posture selection, and data-acquisition
advantages remain to be demonstrated.
1. Introduction
A humanoid can possess a useful motion skill that its autonomous controller cannot reliably invoke. In the setting
studied here, a full-command motor specialist completes eight stopping and corridor tasks when supplied with
detailed commands, whereas an adapter receiving a goal and known scene completes none in the original matched
pilot. The discrepancy matters because the command stream contains decisions that navigation must make: how to
approach, when to slow down, and how to regulate position after reaching a destination. Removing the reference
changes the decision problem rather than merely compressing the input [1, 2].
Motion-tracking foundations provide an attractive control substrate. SONIC exposes learned motor representations
and demonstrates downstream use through a kinematic planner; BFM studies masked online distillation for flexible
behavior specifications [3, 4]. Neither result establishes that arbitrary predicted commands preserve a specific
downstream task. A frozen controller can retain its original behavior under valid commands while responding poorly
to inconsistent commands produced by a learned adapter. Our study therefore separates preservation of the motor
function from the adapter’s ability to select commands that lead to successful execution.

Motion–scene pairing creates a second opportunity and a second ambiguity. A generated scene may be geometrically
compatible with a reference without providing evidence that the robot executed it, stopped successfully, or recovered
after drifting from it. Motion-conditioned environment construction has precedents in learning from hallucination,
and recent humanoid systems pair motions with terrain and distill perceptive skills [5–7]. We investigate how
these resources should be connected to a preserved motor interface without treating geometric compatibility or an
available expert query as a successful navigation demonstration.
The latest increment makes the unresolved question sharper. Structured command and motor-action supervision
greatly improve fitting on recorded teacher states, yet the new policies remain unreliable in the same eight tasks. A
causal velocity input produces one original-panel success, but that success does not repeat in two adaptively selected
follow-up evaluations. Takeover diagnostics additionally show that the nominal continuation works from some
student states and not others. These observations are evidence against promoting nominal-state fitting error as the
principal navigation model-selection signal in this pilot; they do not identify a unique failure mechanism [1].
The current contribution is an implemented motor-preserving navigation interface and an instrumented empirical
study that separates offline fit, unassisted task success, local recovery, and deadline censoring. The proposed next
contribution is execution-qualified supervision on navigation-induced states, followed by a controlled evaluation
of generated motion–scene data. The central hypothesis is that useful downstream transfer depends on task-
compatible coverage of approach, transition, braking, and recovery states, rather than nominal geometric pairing
alone. That hypothesis remains open to comparison with simpler explanations, including observation insufficiency,
command-interface limitations, and inadequate outcome feedback.

2. Related work and the research distinction
Motor foundations and task interfaces. SONIC supports motor reuse through learned representations and
downstream planning [3]. BFM combines masked online distillation and a conditional variational model for flexible
commands [4]. The present adapter does not claim a new universal motor foundation. It uses the recovered local
specialist as a fixed function and studies the information and supervision needed to access its behavior from goal/map
inputs.
Motion-conditioned data and whole-body traversal. Learning from Learned Hallucination learns motion-
conditioned obstacle configurations for ground and aerial navigation [5]. PHP composes atomic motions, trains
tracking experts, and combines DAgger with reinforcement learning to obtain depth-conditioned humanoid skills
[6]. Light-Loco-Parkour expands sparse seed motions into terrain-paired references and explicitly trains transitions
between locomotion and whole-body skills [7]. These are close precedents, not evidence that the present pairing
pipeline is novel. A differentiated contribution requires measuring the learning or acquisition value of this pipeline
against a valid alternative.
Navigation and locomotion teaching. GuideWalk uses a composite navigation/locomotion teacher and subsequent
reinforcement learning with imitation [8]. Collision-Free Humanoid Traversal studies obstacle-dependent whole-
body behavior with a humanoid potential-field representation and hybrid scene generation [9]. Consequently, neither
a hierarchical teacher nor obstacle traversal alone is a sufficient novelty claim here. The intended distinction is
the connection between generated motion–scene supervision, a fixed anticipatory motor interface, and empirically
qualified corrections on student-induced states.
Learner-state supervision and asymmetric information. DAgger addresses the distribution induced by a
learned policy rather than only the demonstration distribution [10]. Asymmetric-learning work separately shows
that a privileged expert can make choices a partially informed learner cannot reliably infer [11]. Our proposed
extension does not replace these principles or inherit their guarantees. It addresses a local implementation issue: a
nominal reference tracker may return an action at a student state without supplying a task-compatible continuation.
Qualification and observation checks must therefore precede strong claims about expert supervision.

3.3 Evidence types and the scientific question
We distinguish a geometric pair (𝑟, 𝐶, 𝑔), an executed teacher episode, a candidate same-state query, and an executed
continuation from a student state. Geometric compatibility does not imply successful dynamics. An executed
nominal task does not prove recovery from arbitrary states. A numerical query does not prove that its continuation
reaches the requested goal [1].
Let 𝑑demo denote the distribution of recorded teacher observations and 𝑑𝜋𝜃the distribution induced by navigation-
generated commands. The measured offline loss estimates fit under the former. Our central question is whether
task-compatible supervision collected under the latter improves stable task completion, and whether generated
motion–scene pairing makes that supervision more valuable or less costly to acquire. Distribution shift and expert
incompatibility are separate hypotheses; neither follows solely from a difference between offline loss and task
success.

4.4 Preservation and query capture
An action-only gradient smoke through the actual encoder, FSQ, and decoder produces a nonzero gradient at the
navigation command head and no inherited-parameter gradients. Each fit checks exact equality of inherited tensors
and an unchanged full-command anchor batch. These checks establish preservation of the bound motor function on
the specified tensors and test inputs; they do not establish that the navigation adapter selects valid commands in
closed loop [1].
NavigationQueryCallback chooses the unassisted navigation action first, then reconstructs a candidate full
command at that physical state and queries the frozen motor. The target is never passed to action inference. Two
archived-policy attempts produce 850 candidate rows with root, speed, and contact parity to the original rollouts.
Every supported-query mask is false, and the candidate schema is rejected by the successful-task loader. No
navigation DAgger fit has been performed on these rows [1].
5. Completed experiments and results
5.1 Comparison contract
The registered comparison trains two new adapters from scratch for 6,000 updates with batch size 256, AdamW
learning rate 10−4, and training seed 91370. Both use the same 3,900 executed rows, frozen motor, exact map, eight
tasks, original deadlines, and evaluation seed 91260. The final registered update is evaluated without task-based
checkpoint selection [1].
The two new conditions differ in the causal localization feature. This is an information-interface comparison within
one small pilot. Comparisons to the archived adapter also change the objective and stopping-tail data, so they cannot
separately identify the effect of either. Eight tasks from four clips in one ancestry group are in-sample observations,
not eight independent motion sources or a held-out scene test.

5.3 What ends a near-successful hold?
The archived 00908 corridor policy maintains 42 valid ticks before speed exceeds the threshold at measurement tick
243. Its distance remains inside the goal. By contrast, the archived 00265 corridor policy’s 38-tick hold is still
intact at the 500-tick deadline; it is a late-arrival failure, not a broken 38-tick hold. Other archived goal entries never
satisfy position and speed jointly even for one tick [1].
This distinction changes the intervention. The first case requires suppressing velocity and subsequent drift. The
second requires earlier arrival or an earlier transition to braking under the unchanged deadline. Treating both as a
generic failure to stand would conflate different task mechanisms.

The first bank should address two measured deficits: earlier braking for 00265-like approaches and position-

space task teacher with the motor fixed, or extend the reference generator. A new student architecture cannot supply
missing expert capability by definition. This is a contingent design choice, not a result that task RL is necessary.
6.2 Separate local stabilization from deadline-qualified task support
For a tested entry state and continuation, store a receipt
𝜌= (state_id, continuation_id, 𝐻, 𝑆local, 𝑆deadline, Δ𝑡stable, contacts, termination).
𝑆local indicates measured stabilization within the declared diagnostic horizon; 𝑆deadline indicates success within the
original task budget. A continuation may be locally successful yet late. The receipt belongs to its tested state, history,
scene, continuation, and horizon. It is neither a safety certificate nor evidence for untested neighboring states.

This yields target-specific admission rather than one eligibility flag. Locally successful late segments can teach
a stabilization subproblem where their observations and task labels are compatible. They must not be counted
as successful original-deadline episodes. Failed or unknown candidate advice remains available for analysis and
sampling, but is not used as positive expert imitation by default. An unavailable expert is not a negative label for the
student action.
Deadlines also introduce an information boundary. If the expert changes its decision based on remaining time, a
student cannot be expected to infer hidden urgency from identical public observations. The first implementation
should keep the expert’s local regulation rule independent of hidden clip time and treat the deadline as an evaluator.
A deliberately deadline-aware policy would require a separately declared public time-to-deadline input and a new
matched observation comparison.
6.3 Supported short-cycle aggregation
Initialize from the declared offline adapter. In each bounded cycle, freeze a driver snapshot and collect unassisted
navigation trajectories. Sample entry states by phase and observed failure, with coverage quotas that prevent selecting
only easy standing states. Validate a finite set of candidate continuations by state-preserving takeover or verified
simulator branching. Record candidate and executed actions separately.
Add admitted query/recovery targets to a separate replay view and fit the existing objective with each term
normalized by its admitted target count. A starting proposal is equal sampling of nominal task replay and supported
recent/recovery targets, followed by an explicit mixture comparison; this ratio is a hypothesis, not a delivered
curriculum. If the supported stratum is empty, do not silently substitute old rows and call the run navigation DAgger.
Evaluate the new student without intervention after every predeclared budget. Log total simulator transitions, expert
queries, branch executions, useful supported decisions, attempted tasks, optimizer updates, wall time, and replay
exposure. Qualification has a cost; any efficiency claim must include failed candidates and validation rollouts.
The effect should be tested against continued offline fitting, fresh nominal-clock querying, and validated-query

The nominal-clock arm is an exploratory simulation comparison with
explicit target uncertainty, not an assumed perfect DAgger expert. A data-volume-matched nominal replay control
distinguishes useful state/target coverage from simply obtaining more rows.
6.4 Contingent architecture and reward comparisons
Two later interventions answer specific unresolved questions. A small recurrent navigation encoder can test whether
causal context history beyond the backward velocity estimate is useful. Sequence training must preserve original
indices, reset masks, burn-in, and the same previous-command convention used in inference. One-step expert
histories cannot be silently substituted for the recurrent student’s own command history.
A direct-reference predictor can test whether the current-command-plus-forecaster interface limits scene-dependent
anticipation. Keep the public input, decoder where feasible, execution budget, and target evidence matched.
Alternatively, task-reward refinement of the command adapter can test whether an outcome-level objective corrects
residual failures despite good imitation. For stochastic command-space optimization, probability ratios must be
computed on the actually sampled command variables, not assumed densities of decoded motor actions. PHP and
GuideWalk motivate this comparison but do not establish its outcome for the present stopping task [6, 8].

7.3 Splits, comparators, and uncertainty
Keep the eight current tasks as a regression suite. Freeze new layouts independently of demonstration trajectories
before training or method selection. Preserve scene families, repaired-motion parents, crops, and source ancestry in
split manifests. Generalization to new layouts with familiar motor skills is a valid claim and should be distinguished
from generalization to new motion ancestry.
Evaluate the full-command specialist on every proposed task as an oracle-command execution control. Report
both total task coverage and navigation transfer conditional on oracle-supported tasks; do not silently remove
oracle failures from the main denominator. This control has richer information and is not an information-matched
autonomous baseline.
Use independent training seeds for training variation and paired evaluation initial states where meaningful. Count
motion ancestry groups, clips, layouts, tasks, episodes, and seeds separately. Choose the final sample size from pilot
variability, desired precision, and the smallest practically meaningful success difference. A fixed count of seeds is
not by itself sufficient for a generalization claim. Confidence estimates should preserve layout/source clustering and
paired comparisons; the current aggregate report does not support constructing missing episode-level intervals.

8. Discussion and limitations
What the pilot establishes. The implemented interface preserves the bound motor path, and the supplied study
distinguishes observation timing, target construction, offline fitting, task success, and takeover outcomes. It
demonstrates that better nominal-state fit did not produce a reliable policy under the tested setting. That is a useful
diagnostic result, not a proof that all offline distillation is inadequate.
What remains unexplained. Navigation-induced state coverage is an important candidate explanation, but the
qualified expert support needed to test it is not yet available. The 850 queries are deliberately unqualified. Missing
temporal information, inconsistent command sequences, task-incompatible nominal references, and a mismatch
between per-step error and terminal success remain competing explanations. The causal velocity comparison is too
narrow and unstable to resolve them.
What is not a downstream scene result. Current clear/corridor tasks do not require alternate routing or low-
clearance posture changes. Four clips in one ancestry group cannot establish broad source diversity. A known map
and zero-delay ideal poses cannot establish sensor-only control. The supplied evaluations run in simulation; there
is no hardware navigation evidence in this packet. Retained normalization and weights do not imply safety under
arbitrary predicted commands [1].
What the integrated paper still needs. The upstream generation procedure and motor-recovery controls must
be documented from their actual artifacts. The existing earlier report provides useful motor-completion counts,
but these are not a substitute for the missing methods and per-motion evidence. The downstream contribution
needs a task family that uses whole-body context, reliable unassisted execution, independent layouts, and a valid
pairing/data-value comparison. None should be filled with anticipated numbers.
9. Conclusion
A strong full-command motor specialist is a necessary resource for the studied interface, but nominal command
execution and navigation competence are different properties.
In an eight-task in-sample study, structured
motor supervision and causal localization reduce recorded teacher-state action error by approximately 13.6–13.7
times without establishing reliable task completion. State-preserving takeovers separate local recoverability,
deadline-limited stabilization, and persistent goal drift. The next methodological step is to qualify task-compatible
continuations from navigation-induced states and test whether their supervision improves unassisted control. The
intended broader contribution is a measured connection from generated motion–scene pairs to useful downstream
whole-body navigation; that connection remains an explicit experimental objective rather than a completed claim.
